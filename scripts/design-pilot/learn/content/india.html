Aadhaar is India’s identification system for residents. The Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI) issues Aadhaar numbers and provides ways to authenticate identity. Organisations using Aadhaar in a service have their own responsibilities for how it is used.

UIDAI states that an Aadhaar number is a proof of identity, not of citizenship or domicile, and that Aadhaar is not in itself a proof of date of birth.

From an identity system to services

Aadhaar can help an organisation establish or verify identity in a process. The system and the services that use it are different parts of the interaction: UIDAI provides identification and authentication infrastructure, while an organisation decides how that fits its own service and applicable rules.

UIDAI also publishes the Aadhaar App. According to UIDAI, an Aadhaar number holder can use the app to share Aadhaar verifiable credentials, in full or in part and with consent, with organisations that verify them offline.

The FIDES catalog includes examples submitted by organisations describing how the Aadhaar App can be used in a service. These examples are useful for exploring the roles involved, but a catalog entry does not establish that every organisation uses the same process.
