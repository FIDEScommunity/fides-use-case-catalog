Organisations regularly exchange company information, permits, certifications and evidence of authority. A [fides_learn_term term="Business wallet"]business wallet[/fides_learn_term] may help an organisation manage or present some of that information. Products differ, so the first question is which interaction you want to support. Start with a process you want to improve, then look at the information and tools it needs.

Start with one transaction

Take supplier onboarding as an example. A customer asks for company information, evidence of a certification and confirmation that a representative may act for the supplier. Map the current process before selecting technology. Which information is requested? Who provides it? Which checks are repeated, and what does the customer do with the result?

This example is illustrative. The information required and the checks to perform depend on the transaction and the parties involved.

Identify the roles

A register, certification body or another party may provide information. The supplier may hold or manage a credential, and the customer's service may receive and check it. These roles are not fixed to particular kinds of organisation. A register is not always the issuer, and an organisation may issue information in one process and receive it in another.

Some business-wallet products can also issue or verify credentials. Others focus on holding and presenting them. These capabilities vary by product. Identify which roles your organisation needs before comparing providers.

Separate identity from authority

Knowing which organisation you are dealing with does not by itself establish what a representative is allowed to do. Ask where the evidence of authority comes from, what it covers, how long it applies and what the receiving party needs to check. The answers depend on the transaction and the applicable rules. See also [fides_learn_term term="Representation rights"]representation[/fides_learn_term] and [fides_learn_term term="Mandate"]mandates[/fides_learn_term].

Check the whole interaction

Consider the credential types, formats and protocols each party supports, and the trust arrangements for the process. Also look at access management, integration with existing systems, deployment and support. A feature listed in a catalog is a starting point for discussion. It does not by itself establish that the complete interaction will work between the parties you have in mind.

How does the European Business Wallet fit?

The European Business Wallet is the subject of a European legislative proposal for organisational and business interactions. Business wallets and related tools also exist outside that proposal. When comparing solutions, distinguish what a product supports today from requirements that may apply under a future European framework. A FIDES listing is not proof of compliance with requirements that have not been finalised. For the proposal and its status, see European Business Wallet. To see how it relates to the EUDI Wallet, see EUDI Wallet and European Business Wallet: how do they relate?.

Prepare a small first step

Choose one transaction, one receiving service and the evidence it needs. Document the current checks and the assumptions you want to test. Then discuss the process with the organisations that provide, hold and receive the information. A readiness workshop or assessment can help your team examine the process and the decisions ahead.
