Organisations regularly exchange company information, permits, certifications and evidence of authority. A [fides_learn_term term="Business wallet"]business wallet[/fides_learn_term] may help an organisation manage or present some of that information. Products differ, so the first questions are which role your organisation plays and which interaction you want to support. This article helps you choose a business wallet. Identify the roles

A register, certification body or another party may provide information. The supplier may hold or manage a credential, and the customer's service may receive and check it. These roles are not fixed to particular kinds of organisation. A register is not always the issuer, and an organisation may issue information in one process and receive it in another.

Some business-wallet products can also issue or verify credentials. Others focus on holding and presenting them. These capabilities vary by product. Identify which roles your organisation needs before comparing providers.

Separate identity from authority

Knowing which organisation you are dealing with does not by itself establish what a representative is allowed to do. Ask where the evidence of authority comes from, what it covers, how long it applies and what the receiving party needs to check. The answers depend on the transaction and the applicable rules. See also [fides_learn_term term="Representation rights"]representation[/fides_learn_term] and [fides_learn_term term="Mandate"]mandates[/fides_learn_term].

Compare wallets and providers

When comparing wallets and providers, consider the credential types, formats and protocols each party supports, and the trust arrangements for the process. Also look at access management, integration with existing systems, deployment and support. A feature listed in a catalog is a starting point for discussion. It does not by itself establish that the complete interaction will work between the parties you have in mind.

How does the European Business Wallet fit?

The European Business Wallet is the subject of a European legislative proposal for organisational and business interactions. Business wallets and related tools also exist outside that proposal. When comparing solutions, distinguish what a product supports today from requirements that may apply under a future European framework. A FIDES listing is not proof of compliance with requirements that have not been finalised. For the proposal and its status, see European Business Wallet. To see how it relates to the EUDI Wallet, see EUDI Wallet and European Business Wallet: how do they relate?.
