Start with one transaction

Take supplier onboarding as an example. A customer asks for company information, evidence of a certification and confirmation that a representative may act for the supplier. Start by writing down the questions the customer needs answered. A wallet can support parts of that process, but the process defines the requirements.

Map the roles

A [fides_learn_term term="Business wallet"]business wallet[/fides_learn_term] may help an organisation keep and present credentials about itself. Some solutions can also issue credentials or check information received from others. These capabilities vary by product. Identify which roles your organisation needs before comparing providers.

Separate identity from authority

Knowing which company you are dealing with does not by itself establish what a representative is allowed to do. Ask where the relevant evidence of authority comes from, what it covers and how the receiving party will assess it. The answer depends on the transaction and its applicable rules. See also [fides_learn_term term="Representation rights"]representation[/fides_learn_term] and [fides_learn_term term="Mandate"]mandates[/fides_learn_term].

Check the whole interaction

Compare the required credential types, supported formats and protocols, and the trust arrangements between the parties. Also consider deployment, access management, integration with existing systems and support. A feature listed in a catalogue is a starting point for a discussion, rather than proof that your complete process will work.

Prepare a small first step

Pick one transaction, one receiving service and the evidence it needs. Document the current manual checks and the assumptions you want to test. This gives your team a practical basis for a demonstration, a provider conversation or a readiness workshop.
