Start with one transaction

Take supplier onboarding as an example. A buyer wants to identify the supplier and check whether the person submitting the application can act for it. Which role does your organisation play?

This example is illustrative. The information required and the checks to perform depend on the transaction and the parties involved.

Choose your role

  • Hold and presentThe supplier keeps proof of company registration in its wallet and shares the details the buyer needs. Under the proposed European Business Wallet framework, the supplier could share its owner identification data ([fides_learn_term term="European Business Wallet Owner Identification Data"]EBWOID[/fides_learn_term]), which includes a unique identifier for the wallet owner.
  • IssueAn issuer gives the supplier a digital credential. For example, a company register can issue proof that the company is registered.
  • Request and verifyThe buyer asks for evidence, checks it and decides whether to open a supplier account.

An organisation can play different roles in different transactions. An issuer can use information from a register or another source. A wallet product may support one role or several.

Compare wallets and providers

Once you know your role in the transaction, look at the part of the process you need to support. Start with your existing systems and see which capability is missing.

  • [fides_learn_term term="Business wallet"]Business wallet[/fides_learn_term]If your organisation needs to hold and share its own evidence, look for a business wallet that supports those credentials and fits your workflow. Business wallets often also support issuing or verifying credentials. Check which functions the product offers.
  • Issuing softwareIf your organisation needs to issue credentials, look for software or a provider that can connect to the source of the data and deliver credentials to the wallets people use.
  • Verification softwareIf your organisation needs to request and check evidence, look for software or a service that can return the result to your existing process.
  • Wallet connectorA wallet connector links an application to digital wallets. The term covers products, services and software development kits (SDKs). Depending on the connector, it can help issue credentials, request and verify them, or do both.
  • [fides_learn_term term="Intermediary"]Intermediair[/fides_learn_term][fides_learn_term term="Intermediary"]Intermediary[/fides_learn_term]If you want a provider to make EUDI Wallet requests on your behalf, consider an intermediary. Under eIDAS, an intermediary acting for a relying party is itself treated as a relying party and may not store data about the content of the transaction. Agree who makes the request, who verifies the evidence and who decides what happens next.

Separate identity from authority

A company acts through its representatives. A director, an employee or someone outside the company may have authority to act on its behalf. In supplier onboarding, the buyer checks which company is applying and whether the person submitting the application can act for it in that transaction.

Some transactions require a person to act on behalf of the company. For example, if a contract requires a qualified electronic signature, an authorised representative signs it as a natural person. Where the signing process supports it, the representative can use their EUDI Wallet to sign. Check separately whether that person has authority to sign this contract for the company.

Knowing which organisation you are dealing with does not by itself establish what a representative is allowed to do. Ask where the evidence of authority comes from, what it covers, how long it applies and what the receiving party needs to check. The answers depend on the transaction and the applicable rules. See also [fides_learn_term term="Representation rights"]representation[/fides_learn_term] and [fides_learn_term term="Mandate"]mandates[/fides_learn_term].

How does the European Business Wallet fit?

The European Business Wallet is a type of business wallet defined in a proposed EU regulation that is still going through the legislative process. Under the proposal, it would give businesses and public bodies a common way to identify themselves, share digital evidence and send documents across borders. It would also let organisations manage who can use the wallet on their behalf. The proposal includes four qualified trust services that support those transactions.

  • Qualified electronic signatureA person can sign a document electronically with the same legal effect as a handwritten signature.
  • Qualified electronic sealHelps establish the origin and integrity of the sealed data.
  • Qualified electronic time stampProvides evidence that the data existed at a particular time.
  • [fides_learn_term term="Qualified Electronic Registered Delivery Service"]Qualified electronic registered delivery service (QERDS)[/fides_learn_term]Provides evidence of who sent and received the data and when. Under eIDAS, the data is presumed to have retained its integrity.

A supplier sends a document to a public authority. QERDS can provide evidence that the authority received it and when. The authority can use separate credentials to check the supplier's identity and the representative's authority.

Follow the proposal and its legislative status in European Business Wallet. For checking the qualified status of a service, see Trust service providers and trust frameworks.
