Your organisation may hold information that people or other organisations regularly need to demonstrate. An education provider confirms a qualification. A professional body confirms a membership. A public authority provides information about a permit.

One option is to issue that information as a digital credential. The recipient can hold it in a wallet and present it when a service asks for relevant information. For the basic terms, see What is a credential? And what is an attestation?

What does an issuer do?

[fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]An issuer[/fides_learn_term] provides a credential containing information it is prepared to confirm.

Before issuing it, the organisation needs to establish what it is confirming and how it obtains or checks the underlying information. The issuer also needs to decide who may receive the credential and under which conditions.

For example, an education provider could issue a credential confirming that a person completed a course. The recipient could then present that credential to an employer or another education provider. This example describes a possible process. The participating organisations would still need to agree what the credential means and how it can be used.

Start with a purpose

A credential is useful when a recipient can use it and another party can understand what it confirms. Before designing one, discuss:

  • Who needs the information, and for which process?
  • Who is the intended [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term]?
  • Which information can your organisation confirm?
  • Which other parties need to understand or check it?
  • Are there existing credential types or agreements that fit the purpose?

These questions can help avoid creating a credential that works for one demonstration but is difficult to use elsewhere.

Where does the information come from?

An issuer may use information from its own records or from another source it is permitted to consult. It needs to determine whether that information is suitable for the claim being made.

The issuer also needs to consider what happens when information changes. Depending on the credential and its use, this can involve expiry, renewal or a way to communicate that it should no longer be relied on. Not every credential uses the same lifecycle. The approach should fit the information and the agreements around its use.

What do recipients and relying parties need to know?

The wallet holder needs to understand what they are receiving and when they may use it.

The organisation receiving a presentation, the [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term], needs to understand what the credential confirms, who issued it and how to check it. Depending on the ecosystem, these details may be set out in a schema, a rulebook or other agreements.

FIDES uses the term credential type for a description of a kind of credential. A type can help parties find out what information is defined and where further specifications can be found.

What about EUDI Wallets?

Within the European digital identity framework, some attributes can be issued as [fides_learn_term term="Electronic Attestation of Attributes"]electronic attestations of attributes[/fides_learn_term]. Different categories and requirements apply, including for qualified attestations.

An organisation considering issuance for EUDI Wallets should establish which category applies to its information, whether it is authorised to issue it and which European and national requirements are relevant. It should also check what wallet and credential support is available for the intended process. A listing in a FIDES catalog does not settle any of these points.

Build, use existing software or work with a provider?

Issuance requires organisational decisions as well as software. An organisation may develop components itself, use existing software or work with a provider. Whichever route it chooses, the organisation remains responsible for understanding its role, the information it confirms and the arrangements under which the credential is issued.

Choose one credential and one intended use. Discuss them with a prospective holder and a relying party before deciding how to implement it.
