When organisations exchange credentials, they need more than a way to send data. A receiving service needs to know which issuers or other participants it can rely on and which rules apply to the interaction. Trust and interoperability introduces the idea; this article looks at frameworks and registries.

What is a trust framework?

A trust framework sets out agreements and rules for participants in an ecosystem. Depending on its purpose, it may describe eligibility, responsibilities, technical requirements, how organisations are recognised and how disputes or incidents are handled.

Frameworks differ. A framework for a sector, a national scheme and an international arrangement can each define different requirements. Read the rules that apply to the specific interaction. For the link between a framework and trust services, see Trust service providers and trust frameworks.

What can a registry provide?

A registry can make information about participants, services, identifiers or other resources available. The information it contains, who maintains it and what a listing means depend on its purpose and governance. The W3C data model calls one such role a verifiable data registry and gives trusted databases, government ID databases and distributed ledgers as examples.

A registry entry can help a service find information for a check. A service still needs to understand what the registry establishes, whether the information is current and what additional checks apply.

A concrete example is the trust model in the Architecture and Reference Framework of the European Digital Identity Wallet. Among other things, it describes that providers of person identification data and attestations are registered by a registrar before they interact with a wallet, and that trust anchors are published in [fides_learn_term term="Trusted List"]trusted lists[/fides_learn_term] or [fides_learn_term term="List of Trusted Entities"]lists of trusted entities[/fides_learn_term]. Other frameworks and registries are organised differently.

Trusted lists and qualified trust services

Under the EU eIDAS framework, EU countries establish, maintain and publish national trusted lists of qualified trust service providers and the qualified services they provide. According to the European Commission, a provider and its services are qualified only if they appear in a trusted list. When checking qualified status, consult the applicable trusted list and check the specific provider and service at the relevant time.

The FIDES organisation catalog can help you discover providers and filter for QTSP information. Use the official trusted lists for an authoritative check of qualified status. Trust service providers and trust frameworks explains what qualified means.

Questions to ask

  • Who maintains the framework or registry, and how is information updated?
  • What does inclusion establish, and what does it leave for participants to check?
  • How does a service determine which information is current and applicable?
  • What happens when a participant or service loses its status?
