In the United States, digital identity developments include [fides_learn_term term="Mobile Driving Licence Credential"]mobile driving licences[/fides_learn_term] offered by individual states and other jurisdictions. Availability and acceptance vary by jurisdiction and by the organisation checking the licence.

A licence on a phone

NIST describes a mobile driving licence as a digital form of a driving licence that can be presented from a phone. NIST notes that states and territories issue the licences and that several standards organisations are involved. Some airports accept participating mobile licences. The Transportation Security Administration (TSA) lists the participating states and wallets and says travellers should still carry a physical ID. Its list, checked on 13 November 2026, also shows that acceptance can end: it says Utah’s mobile licence will no longer be accepted from 1 January 2027 and West Virginia’s from 4 May 2027. Check the relevant state and the organisation requesting identification for current availability and requirements.

A service may use the information in a mobile licence to check an attribute, such as whether a person meets an age requirement. The California DMV, for example, lists age-restricted retail purchases among the uses of its mobile licence in the CA DMV Wallet. The information requested and the technical interaction depend on the service and the implementation.
