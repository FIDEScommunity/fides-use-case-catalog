A credential format specifies how information and its associated proof are represented. A receiving service needs to understand the format to process the credential and carry out the relevant checks. For a first explanation, see Formats and protocols: what do they do?; this article looks at what a format describes and why a matching format is not enough.

What does a format describe?

A credential can contain information about a person, an organisation or another subject. It also contains or refers to information that helps a receiving party establish who issued it and check whether it has been altered. The details depend on the format and the way the credential is secured.

[fides_learn_term term="Verifiable Credentials Data Model"]The W3C Verifiable Credentials Data Model[/fides_learn_term] describes a data model for verifiable credentials; version 2.0 is a W3C Recommendation (May 2025). [fides_learn_term term="SD-JWT-VC"]SD-JWT VC[/fides_learn_term] and [fides_learn_term term="mDL/mDoc"]mdoc[/fides_learn_term] are examples of approaches encountered in wallet ecosystems. They do not all use identical structures or mechanisms.

SD-JWT VC is described in an IETF Internet-Draft. Its working group has submitted it to the IESG for publication, but it remains an Internet-Draft (version 26 when checked on 13 November 2026). The mdoc format is specified in ISO/IEC 18013-5, a standard for mobile driving licences that ISO lists as due for revision. Check the applicable specification and version for each implementation.

More than a format

Two products may list support for the same format but still be unable to complete a particular interaction. They may support different versions, credential types, protocols or ways of establishing trust, and they may apply different rules for status checks or presentation.

Choices for one environment can be brought together in an [fides_learn_term term="Interop Profile"]interoperability profile[/fides_learn_term]. The scope of a profile depends on the organisation or ecosystem that defines it. Formats and protocols: what do they do? explains how profiles relate to formats and protocols.

Comparing products

Start with an actual credential and interaction, and use the questions in Formats and protocols: what do they do?. Also ask how the issuer is recognised and how status is checked, then test the interaction, including what happens when information is missing or a check fails.

The FIDES catalogs provide information that can help identify products and their reported features. Confirm current support with the relevant providers and test it for your use case.
