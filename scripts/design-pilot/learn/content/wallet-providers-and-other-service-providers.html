What does a wallet provider do?

A wallet provider offers a wallet and the services needed to use it. Depending on the product and its operating model, that can include onboarding users, maintaining the software, providing support and helping users manage access to their wallet.

A personal wallet is intended for an individual. A business wallet can support an organisation in managing and exchanging information in a business process. Products differ in their intended users, functions and the credentials they support.

How does a wallet connect to other parties?

An issuer needs a way to provide a credential to the wallet. A relying party needs a way to request and receive information from it. The wallet, issuer and receiving service need compatible ways of communicating, as well as agreements about the interaction.

A wallet provider may build its own software or work with other suppliers. The organisation providing the wallet, the developer and the organisation responsible for operating it are not necessarily the same.

Other providers in the interaction

An issuer or relying party can use a technology provider to connect its systems. A provider may supply issuing software, verification tools or integration services. Which tasks it performs depends on the agreement with its customer and the design of the service.

Choosing a wallet or provider

Start with the process and the people or organisations involved. Consider what information needs to be exchanged, which devices or systems are used, how access is managed and what support is available. Check the relevant formats, protocols and arrangements for the intended interaction with the provider.

The FIDES catalogs help you discover wallets and their reported features. Check current product information and whether it meets the requirements of your use case with the provider.
