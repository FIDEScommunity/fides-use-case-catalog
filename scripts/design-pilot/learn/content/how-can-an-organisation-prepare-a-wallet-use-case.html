Work through one transaction

In this example, a supplier applies for an account. The buyer tests whether evidence shared from a wallet is enough to move the application to the next step. The test covers two checks: which company is applying and whether the representative may submit the application on its behalf.

  1. Define the decision. Can the application move to the next step? For this test, the buyer needs to identify the supplier and check whether the representative may submit the application.
  2. Agree what evidence to use. For company identity, the buyer might accept registration details from a trusted company register. For the representative’s authority, the buyer needs evidence that covers submitting this application. Agree where each piece of evidence comes from, who shares it and who checks it.
  3. Test two applications. In the first, the registration details are valid and the authority covers submitting the application. It should move to the next step. In the second, the evidence of authority has expired. It should go to manual review. Agree in advance who handles that review.

Plan your first test

Choose a transaction from your own process. Copy this sentence into your project document and complete it with your team.

For [transaction], we will test whether [evidence] is enough to make [decision] and agree that [role] will take [alternative action] if the check fails.

Agree how to run the test

Once you have agreed what to test, decide which software, credential format and protocol you will use. Agree which issuers the receiving service will trust and who will handle support during the test. If you also need proof that a document was sent and received, record that as a separate requirement.
