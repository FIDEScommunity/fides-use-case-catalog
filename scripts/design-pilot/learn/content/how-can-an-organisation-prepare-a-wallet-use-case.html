Choose one interaction

Pick a situation that occurs regularly. For example, an employer asks applicants to show a qualification. Describe who has the information, who issues it, who holds it and who needs to check it.

These roles can be carried out by different organisations. A provider may also supply software or support part of the interaction.

This article is about preparing any wallet use case. If you are starting from your organisation's own wallet, see Business wallets: where to start. If your organisation mainly receives information, see Accepting information from a wallet.

Understand the information

Identify the decision the receiving service needs to make and the information it needs for that decision. Find out who is responsible for the source, how current it needs to be and what should happen if it is incorrect.

Consider people who cannot or do not want to use a wallet, and how they can complete the process. For example, a recital of the European Digital Identity Regulation says that users should be under no obligation to use a European Digital Identity Wallet to access private services.

Discuss the practical arrangements

Ask the parties involved how information can be issued and received, which formats and protocols are supported, and what agreements are needed. A shared format alone does not establish that two products will work together in your process.

Also consider who provides support, how problems are resolved and how the service will be maintained. Test assumptions with the people and organisations who will use the process.
