From receiving to sharing

An education provider issues a digital qualification to a student. The student receives it in a wallet. Later, an employer asks for evidence of that qualification. The student can use the wallet to respond to the request.

The credential contains information about a subject. In this example, the student is both the [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term] and the subject. That relationship can differ in other situations. An organisation, for example, may hold a credential containing information about the organisation itself.

Choosing what to share

A wallet can show what information a service asks for. Depending on the wallet, credential and interaction, the holder may be able to share only the requested information. The holder should be able to understand who is requesting it and why before deciding whether to proceed.

If a credential expires, is withdrawn or contains incorrect information, the holder may need to contact its [fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]issuer[/fides_learn_term] or obtain a new credential. The available steps depend on the credential and the service that issued it.

Acting for an organisation

A person may use a wallet in a business process. The information presented might identify the person, the organisation, or both. Whether the person is authorised to act for the organisation is a separate question. A [fides_learn_term term="Mandate"]mandate[/fides_learn_term] or another form of evidence may be relevant to that decision.
