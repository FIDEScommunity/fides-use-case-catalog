A car rental company might ask for licence details before you pick up the vehicle. A service that asks for information from your wallet is called a [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term].

Check the request

Before you respond, look at who is asking, what information is requested and why it is needed. If the request is unexpected or you do not recognise the service, check with the organisation through a channel you already trust.

Sharing online or with a nearby reader

A rental website can ask you to share licence details while you’re still at home. The request and your response travel over the internet. This is called a remote flow.

At a rental desk, your wallet can also share details directly with a compatible reader nearby. This is called a proximity flow. In either case, check who is asking and which details your wallet is about to share.

What can you choose to share?

Some wallets and credentials let you share particular details without sharing every detail in the credential. The options depend on the wallet, the credential and the service. Your wallet should show the information you are about to share.

In an EU Digital Identity Wallet, review the request before you decide whether to share. The details you can share depend on the credential and the options your wallet offers.

What happens afterwards?

The receiving service can check the information it receives. It then decides how to use it for its own process. If you have a question about that use, contact the organisation that received the information. Its privacy information should explain how it handles personal data.
