A car rental company might ask for licence details before you pick up the vehicle. A service that asks for information from your wallet is called a [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term].

Check the request

Before you respond, look at who is asking, what information is requested and why it is needed. If the request is unexpected or you do not recognise the service, check with the organisation through a channel you already trust.

If you cannot confirm who is asking, or the request asks for more information than you expected, pause before approving it. Ask the service why it needs the extra details or whether there is another way to continue.

In an EUDI Wallet, the service’s access certificate helps the wallet check who is asking. The registration certificate tells the wallet why the service registered and which data it registered to request.

Sharing online or with a nearby reader

A rental website can ask you to share licence details while you’re still at home. The request and your response travel over the internet. This is called a remote flow.

At a rental desk, your wallet can also share details directly with a compatible reader nearby. This is called a proximity flow. In either case, check who is asking and which details your wallet is about to share.

What can you choose to share?

Met [fides_learn_term term="Selective Disclosure"]Selective disclosure[/fides_learn_term] lets you share only the details needed for a particular check. With a supported credential, you can confirm you are 18 or over while keeping your date of birth private. When you send a copy of an ID, you may have to cover up details the service does not need. With selective disclosure, the wallet sends the selected details in a form the service can verify.

What happens afterwards?

The receiving service will check the proof you share. It then decides how to use it for its own process. If you have a question about that use, contact the organisation that received the information. Its privacy information should explain how it handles personal data.

Review what you shared

After the wallet has checked the request, you can review the interaction in your EUDI Wallet dashboard.

An EUDI Wallet has a dashboard where you can see which services you connected to and, where applicable, what data you exchanged. A deletion request goes through the dashboard to the receiving service, which handles the request. Article 5a(4)(d) describes the functions for viewing, deletion requests, and reporting a relying party in case of a suspected unlawful or suspicious request.
