A service may ask you to share information from your wallet. For example, an employer may ask for proof that you completed a course. The organisation asking for information is sometimes called a [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term].

Check the request

Before you respond, look at who is asking, what information is requested and why it is needed. If the request is unexpected or you do not recognise the service, check with the organisation through a channel you already trust.

What can you choose to share?

Some wallets and credentials let you share particular details without sharing every detail in the credential. The options depend on the wallet, the credential and the service. Your wallet should show the information you are about to share.

For European Digital Identity Wallets, the European framework provides for users to control the information they share. Check the wallet and service you use for the options available in your situation.

What happens afterwards?

The receiving service can check the information it receives. It then decides how to use it for its own process. If you have a question about that use, contact the organisation that received the information. Its privacy information should explain how it handles personal data.
