One example

A training provider confirms that you completed a course. You keep that confirmation in a wallet. When you apply for a role, the employer asks to see it and checks it. The wallet software comes from a wallet provider, and agreements or registers can help the employer decide which issuers to recognise.

  1. IssuerTraining provider
  2. Holder + walletRecipient
  3. Relying partyEmployer

The example is illustrative. How credentials connect issuers, wallets and services follows it step by step.

The roles at a glance

Start with the first article.

  • How credentials connect issuers, wallets and servicesFollow one example from issue to use.
  • The holderReceives information, keeps it in a wallet and chooses what to share.
  • The issuerProvides a credential and is responsible for what it confirms.
  • The relying partyAsks for information for a purpose and decides what to do with the result.
  • Wallet providers and other service providersSupply software and services that can support an interaction. Responsibilities differ per setup.
  • Trust service providers and trust frameworksAgreements, registers and services that help parties decide what to recognise.

Roles can overlap

One organisation can take more than one role. It may issue credentials in one service and receive them in another. A wallet provider is not automatically the issuer of a credential, and the party that receives information is not in every context the same as the software that checks it.

The EUDI Wallet and the European Business Wallet each have their own purpose and legal framework, so their roles should not be treated as the same. EUDI Wallet and European Business Wallet: how do they relate? compares them.
