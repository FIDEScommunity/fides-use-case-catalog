Organisations regularly need to prove who they are, provide company information or show that someone is authorised to act for them. This can mean collecting the same documents and completing similar checks for each new customer, supplier or public service.

The European Commission has proposed a framework for European Business Wallets. The proposal aims to give organisations a way to identify themselves and exchange trusted information across the EU.

What is the status?

The European Business Wallet is a legislative proposal. It is not yet in force.

The European Commission published its proposal in 2025. The Council adopted its negotiating position on 9 June 2026, and in October 2026 the European Parliament decided to open negotiations with the Council. The final rules and their implementation may differ from the proposal and the negotiating positions. No obligation for organisations follows from the proposal itself.

This is different from the European Digital Identity Wallet, which rests on a framework that has already been adopted. See the European Digital Identity Wallet page.

What could a European Business Wallet be used for?

Under the proposal, an organisation could, for example:

  • provide information about its legal identity;
  • share a permit, certificate or other business document;
  • show that a person is authorised to represent it;
  • use electronic signatures, seals or timestamps;
  • exchange information with another organisation or a public authority.

These capabilities depend on the final rules, the available services and how organisations implement them.

A possible business process

Consider a company that wants to become a supplier to a customer in another EU country. The customer needs to establish which organisation it is dealing with. It may also need company registration information and evidence that the person completing the process is authorised to do so.

A business wallet could support the exchange of that information. The customer would still need to decide which checks its process requires and how to handle the result.

Sending a document to a public authority

Imagine your company needs to submit a document to a public authority in another EU country. Start by checking what the procedure requires. You may need to show which company is submitting the document, who is authorised to act for it, and when the document was sent or received.

The European Commission’s proposal includes several functions that could support a transaction like this:

  • Qualified electronic signature — A person can sign the document electronically.
  • Qualified electronic seal — An organisation can use a seal to support checks on where the document came from and whether it has changed.
  • Qualified electronic timestamp — A timestamp can provide evidence that the document existed at a particular time.
  • Qualified electronic registered delivery — The service can provide evidence of when the document was sent and received.

Which functions you need depends on the procedure. The final rules will determine how European Business Wallets support these transactions.

How does this relate to business wallets already in use?

Business wallets and related tools already exist. Organisations and providers use them in pilots and services for activities such as company onboarding, representation and the exchange of business credentials.

A European Business Wallet would be subject to the requirements established under the proposed European framework. The existence of a business wallet today does not, by itself, mean that it meets future European Business Wallet requirements.

The FIDES Explorer lists business wallets and use cases using the information available for each listing. Inclusion does not mean FIDES has assessed a wallet against future requirements.

Where should an organisation start?

Start with a process in which your organisation repeatedly provides or checks information. Identify the parties involved, what each party needs to know and where the information originates.

You can then look at credentials, wallets and services used in similar processes. A workshop or assessment can help your team examine the choices before selecting a solution.
