The European Digital Identity Wallet and the proposed European Business Wallet address related needs.

The EUDI Wallet is part of the European digital identity framework. It is intended to help users identify themselves and share credentials with services.

The European Business Wallet proposal focuses on how organisations identify themselves, exchange business information and act in business or administrative processes. It is a legislative proposal, not a rule in force. The final rules may differ from the proposal.

The European Commission states that the proposed Business Wallet architecture will build on the architecture and features of the EUDI Wallet.

Three situations

1. A person identifies themselves

A person wants to access an online service. The service needs to know who they are or check a particular attribute.

The person may use an EUDI Wallet to present the information requested by the service. The service is the [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term]: it receives and checks the information for that purpose.

2. An organisation shares business information

A company applies to become a supplier. The customer needs information about the company, such as its registration or a relevant permit.

A business wallet could support the exchange of that information. The parties still need to agree which information is required and whether the source is acceptable for their process.

3. A person acts for an organisation

A person completes a process on behalf of a company. The receiving organisation may need to establish both who the person is and whether they are authorised to act for that company.

This brings together personal identity, organisational information and representation. The exact credentials and interaction depend on the process and on the applicable technical and legal framework.

Are they the same wallet?

They address related needs, but the terms are not interchangeable.

The EUDI Wallet operates within the European digital identity framework. The European Business Wallet is the subject of a separate proposal focused on organisational and business interactions. Business wallets that exist today are not automatically European Business Wallets.

A person acting for an organisation may encounter both personal and organisational information in one process. That does not mean every business process will use the same wallet arrangement.

What should organisations look at first?

Choose one interaction and map the information each party needs:

  1. Who starts the process?
  2. Which person or organisation needs to be identified?
  3. What information or authority must be demonstrated?
  4. Who can provide that information?
  5. Who receives it and what decision follows?

Use those answers to identify which credentials, wallet capabilities and services are relevant to the interaction.
