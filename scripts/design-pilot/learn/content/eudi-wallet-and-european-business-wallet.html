When a company signs up with a new customer, the customer may need to establish who is applying, whether the company is registered and who can act on its behalf.

Consider a supplier completing a customer’s onboarding form. The same online process can bring together information about a person, a company and the authority to act for it.

Three situations

1. A person identifies themselves

The employee completing the form can use an EUDI Wallet to share the identity information the customer needs, if the service supports it.

2. A company provides business information

The customer may need the supplier’s registration details or a permit. A business wallet could help the supplier share that evidence with the customer.

3. A person acts for the company

Before the employee signs an agreement, the customer may also need evidence that the employee can sign for the company. Identifying the employee and checking company registration each answer part of the customer’s question. Authority to sign needs its own evidence.

Are they the same wallet?

The EUDI Wallet is part of the EU framework under eIDAS. The European Business Wallet is the subject of a separate legislative proposal focused on how organisations identify themselves, exchange information and act in business or administrative processes. The proposal builds on the EUDI Wallet architecture. Existing business wallet products can already support some of these tasks, with capabilities that vary by product. The European Parliament procedure file stood at “Awaiting committee decision”.

What should organisations look at first?

Take one decision in your process, such as approving a supplier or accepting a signature. Write down what the recipient needs to know about the person, the organisation and the authority to act. Then check who can provide each piece of evidence.
