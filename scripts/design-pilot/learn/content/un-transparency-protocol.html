[fides_learn_term term="UN Transparency Protocol"]The UN Transparency Protocol[/fides_learn_term] (UNTP) is published by UN/CEFACT as a protocol for digital information about products and supply chains. According to its site, it is a protocol, not a platform: it sets standards for product, facility, traceability, conformity and identity data that any technology platform can support.

Its specifications include a [fides_learn_term term="Digital Product Passport"]digital product passport[/fides_learn_term] and other types of information that can be exchanged across supply chains, such as conformity credentials, traceability events and facility records.

What is the UNTP digital product passport?

In UNTP, the digital product passport is issued by the shipper of goods. It carries basic product and packaging data, together with quality, safety and sustainability performance, for serialised product items or product batches shipped between actors in a value chain. It links to conformity credentials and traceability events.

The UNTP passport is a different thing from the EU regulatory digital product passport. The UNTP page describes it as upstream business-to-business data that can help issue national passports.

The specification describes the passport as a verifiable credential. The product information and the credential that carries it serve different purposes: one describes the product, while the other supports checking who issued the information and whether it has been altered.

Status

UNTP is still under development. The specification page consulted on 13 November 2026 is labelled “Work in Progress” and says it is suitable for pre-production pilot implementations. Its schema and sample files are version 0.8.0. The public review has closed, and the UNTP team says it expects to release version 1.0. Consult the current specification for its status and intended use.
