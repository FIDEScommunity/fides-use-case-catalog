Check the credential

Depending on the credential and the process, a service may need to check who issued it, whether its proof is valid, whether it has been altered and whether it is within its validity period. Status information may indicate that a credential has been withdrawn, where a status mechanism is provided and can be checked.

In W3C terms, verification covers whether a credential is an authentic and current statement of the issuer: the securing mechanism is satisfied and, if a status is present, the [fides_learn_term term="Credential Status"]status[/fides_learn_term] check succeeds. For qualified electronic attestations of attributes, the European Digital Identity Regulation provides that a revoked attestation loses its validity from the moment of revocation.

A service may also need to determine whether the person presenting the information is entitled to use it. How that check works depends on the technology and the situation. Whether a person may act for an organisation is a separate question; see How can someone act for an organisation?.

Decide whether the information is enough

A valid credential can contain information that does not meet a service's requirements. For example, an employer may receive a valid qualification that does not cover a required skill. The service needs its own criteria for deciding what to accept and what to do if a check fails or information is missing.

W3C makes the same distinction: verifying a credential does not establish that the claims in it are true. A verifier validates the claims against its own rules, and relies on them only after evaluating the issuer, the proof, the subject and the claims.

Make the request clear

Explain who is asking for the information and why. Ask for information needed for the process, taking the applicable rules into account. Depending on the wallet and credential, a person may be able to share selected information rather than every attribute in a credential.

For European Digital Identity Wallets, the Regulation requires a relying party to register in the Member State where it is established, to state its intended use of wallets including the data it will request, and to identify itself to the user. A relying party may not request data other than what it indicated at registration, and it is responsible for authenticating and validating the data it receives.
