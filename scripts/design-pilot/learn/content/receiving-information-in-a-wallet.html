A school, public authority or other organisation may offer information that you can receive in a digital wallet. It might be a qualification, a permit or another statement about you. The organisation providing it is called the [fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]issuer[/fides_learn_term].

Your official identity in a wallet

In an EUDI Wallet, Person Identification Data (PID) is the core credential you use to prove your official identity. It contains official identity details such as your name and date of birth. A designated PID provider issues it to your wallet.

Other countries can use different digital identity credentials. In California, for example, people can add a digital driver’s licence or state ID to a wallet and show it where it is accepted.

Add a credential to your wallet

An issuer may offer you a credential through its website or app. For example, you may get a link to open in your wallet or a QR code to scan. Review who is issuing the credential and what it says. If the details are correct and you want to keep it, follow the prompts in your wallet to add it. If you cannot open the offer, check with the issuer which wallets it supports.

Before issuing PID, the PID provider can ask for a [fides_learn_term term="Wallet Unit Attestation"]Wallet Unit Attestation[/fides_learn_term]. The wallet provider supplies this attestation so the PID provider can verify the wallet that will receive your PID.

Open your wallet to see which credentials you hold. Select a credential to see its details. Many wallets show credentials as cards, much like the cards in a physical wallet.

What happens if information changes?

Some credentials expire. An issuer can also withdraw or replace a credential when the information changes. How you receive a replacement depends on the issuer and your wallet. If a service says your credential is no longer valid, follow the instructions from your wallet or the issuer.
