From information to a credential

Before issuing a credential, an issuer needs a way to establish the information it contains. The source may be its own records or information from another organisation. The issuer also needs to determine who should receive the credential and how it will reach their wallet.

A digital signature or other proof can help a recipient check the credential and identify its issuer. It does not, by itself, establish that every statement in the credential is correct or suitable for a particular decision.

After issuance

Information can change. A qualification may remain valid, while a role or authorisation may end. An issuer therefore needs to consider how recipients can check status, when a credential should be replaced and how errors can be corrected. The options depend on the credential and the arrangements used.

One organisation, different roles

An organisation that issues credentials may also receive and check credentials issued by others. The role describes what it does in a particular interaction.
