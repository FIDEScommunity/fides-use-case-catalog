Imagine you are applying for a role that requires a particular qualification. Today you might upload a certificate and wait while the employer checks it. With a digital credential, the issuing training provider can give you a form of evidence that another service can check electronically.

Follow the information

[fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]The issuer[/fides_learn_term] creates the credential. You receive and manage it as the [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term], often using a wallet. When you present it, the employer acts as the verifier, or [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term]. In this example the qualification is about you, so you are also the subject. The parties need compatible arrangements for issuing, holding and checking the credential.

  1. IssuerTraining provider
  2. Holder + walletRecipient
  3. Relying partyEmployer

The example is illustrative.

The issuer provides the credential

The issuer makes a statement it is prepared to confirm. In this example, the training provider confirms that you completed a course. It needs to establish what it is confirming, who may receive the credential and how the information is kept accurate. Depending on the credential and the agreements around it, there may also be a validity period or status information.

The holder receives and presents it

When a service asks for information, you can review the request and decide whether to present it. Depending on the credential and the interaction, you may be able to share selected information. That capability is not a property of every wallet or credential. What can be reused with more than one service depends on the credential and the service.

The receiving service checks it

The employer asks for information and uses the result in its process. A verifier component or service performs technical checks on the presentation. Depending on the credential and its supporting infrastructure, those checks can establish whether the information was altered, who secured it, whether it is within its validity period and whether status information is available.

Those technical checks are distinct from deciding whether the issuer is suitable for the claim or whether the qualification meets the requirements of the role. That decision stays with the employer.

The roles can differ

The roles do not always belong to the same kinds of organisation. A public authority might issue information that a person presents to a company. In another process, one organisation might issue information that another organisation receives. An organisation can also take more than one role: it may issue credentials for one service and receive credentials in another.
