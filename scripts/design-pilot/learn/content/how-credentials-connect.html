Start with the question

Imagine you are applying for a role that requires a particular qualification. Today you might upload a certificate and wait while the employer checks it. With a digital credential, the issuing training provider can give you a form of evidence that another service can check electronically.

Follow the information

The [fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]issuer[/fides_learn_term] creates the credential. You receive and manage it as the [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term], often using a wallet. When you present it, the employer acts as the verifier, or [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term]. In this example the qualification is about you, so you are also the subject. These roles do not have to belong to the same organisations in every use case.

What is checked?

Depending on the credential and its supporting infrastructure, checks can establish whether the information was altered, who secured it, and whether it is within its validity period. Status information may also be available. Those technical checks are distinct from deciding whether an issuer is suitable for the claim or whether the evidence meets a service's requirements.

What changes for the person?

You may be able to use the same credential with more than one service. What can be reused, and what you need to share, depends on the credential and the service. Selective disclosure can limit the information presented when it is supported; it is not a property of every wallet interaction.

Try it in your context

Choose one piece of evidence your organisation asks for. Who could issue it? Who would hold it? What must the receiving service check?
