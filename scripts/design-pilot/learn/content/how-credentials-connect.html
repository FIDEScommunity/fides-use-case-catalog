Suppose you book with a rental company that accepts mobile driving licences. The licence comes from a licensing authority, you share the information it asks for, and the company uses the result to decide whether to rent you the car.

Follow the information

The licensing authority is the [fides_learn_term term="Issuer"]issuer[/fides_learn_term]. You’re the [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term] and use a wallet to manage and share the credential. The rental company is the [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term] because it uses the information to make a decision.

  1. Licensing authorityIssues a digital driving licence to the driver.
  2. DriverThe rental company asks for licence details during booking. The driver reviews the request in their wallet and chooses whether to share the details.
  3. Rental companyChecks the information it receives and decides whether to approve the rental.

The same roles apply if you share licence details with a reader at the rental desk. See Sharing information from a wallet for both ways to share.

The issuer provides the credential

The licensing authority issues the digital licence. The credential can include details such as the categories of vehicle you’re entitled to drive and when the licence expires.

The holder receives and presents it

When the rental company asks for licence details, you can review the request in your wallet before deciding whether to share them. What you can share depends on the credential, wallet and service.

The receiving service checks it

The company can use a verifier component or a verification service to check the credential. Depending on the credential and available status information, the checks can cover its source, whether it has been altered, and whether it is still valid. The rental company decides whether it accepts the licence for this booking and whether you meet its other rental requirements.

Signing in and sharing evidence

When you sign in through an identity provider, the rental site can use the sign-in result to recognise your account. The driving licence answers a different question about which vehicles you can drive. The booking can involve both.

The roles can differ

The roles do not always belong to the same kinds of organisation. A public authority might issue information that a person presents to a company. In another process, one organisation might issue information that another organisation receives. An organisation can also take more than one role: it may issue credentials for one service and receive credentials in another.
