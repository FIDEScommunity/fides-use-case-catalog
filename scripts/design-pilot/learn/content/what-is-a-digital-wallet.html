You may know wallets that hold payment cards or tickets. This page looks at wallets used for digital identity and credentials: information that can be presented to another party, such as proof of a qualification or information about an organisation.

What might a wallet hold?

A [fides_learn_term term="Wallet"]wallet[/fides_learn_term] could hold a digital [fides_learn_term term="Credential"]credential[/fides_learn_term] issued by an education provider, an organisation or a public authority. The information it can receive depends on the wallet, the credential and the arrangements between the parties involved. For what a credential is, see What is a credential? And what is an attestation?

For example, a course provider could issue proof that a person completed a course. The person could receive it in a compatible wallet and later present it to an employer. The employer would decide what it needs to check and whether it accepts the issuer for that purpose.

This is an illustrative example. Not every course provider issues credentials, and not every employer or wallet supports this interaction. The roles in this example are described in How credentials connect issuers, wallets and services.

How does sharing work?

A service can ask the wallet [fides_learn_term term="Holder"]holder[/fides_learn_term] for information. The holder can then review the request and decide whether to share it. Depending on the wallet, credential and service, it may be possible to share selected information rather than every detail in a credential. This is called [fides_learn_term term="Selective Disclosure"]selective disclosure[/fides_learn_term].

The receiving service must still check the information and decide whether it meets its requirements. A wallet does not make that decision for the organisation. For the checks a [fides_learn_term term="Relying Party"]relying party[/fides_learn_term] makes, see Accepting information from a wallet. For the organisations that provide credentials, see Issuing credentials for wallets.

Personal wallets and business wallets

A personal wallet is used by a person. It may help that person receive and present information about themselves, such as an identity credential or a qualification.

A business wallet is intended for an organisation's activities. Depending on the product, it may help manage information about the organisation, support representatives or take part in issuing and checking credentials.

The distinction is useful when comparing products, but the roles can overlap. A person may use their own wallet while acting for an organisation, and business-wallet products do not all offer the same functions. If you are looking at business wallets for your organisation, Business wallets: where to start suggests how to begin.

Are all wallets compatible?

No. Wallets and services can support different credential formats, protocols and trust arrangements. Whether a particular credential can be received or presented depends on the combination of issuer, wallet, receiving service and rules they use.

Before choosing a wallet, identify the process you want to support and the parties it needs to work with. Use the list of supported features to discuss whether the wallet fits that process.

What about the EUDI Wallet and the European Business Wallet?

The [fides_learn_term term="European Digital Identity Wallet"]EUDI Wallet[/fides_learn_term] is part of the European digital identity framework. The [fides_learn_term term="European Business Wallet"]European Business Wallet[/fides_learn_term] is the subject of a separate European legislative proposal focused on organisational and business interactions. Their relationship, and the requirements applying to each, are explained in the European wallet articles: European Digital Identity Wallet, European Business Wallet and EUDI Wallet and European Business Wallet: how do they relate?

Other wallet ecosystems exist outside these initiatives and outside Europe. FIDES includes examples from different countries and sectors in the Explorer.
