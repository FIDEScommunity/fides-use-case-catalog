Germany is developing a state EUDI Wallet and an ecosystem in which organisations can issue credentials or offer services that use wallet information. The programme publishes information for the public on its official website, and its FAQ explains how organisations can test use cases in a sandbox.

The name and the planned launch

On 9 September 2026, the Federal Minister for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation, Karsten Wildberger, presented the name of the government-backed [fides_learn_term term="European Digital Identity Wallet"]EUDI Wallet[/fides_learn_term] app: d-you. The programme’s news item about this is dated 15 September 2026. It says the first credentials and use cases are scheduled to become available when d-you launches on 2 January 2027, and that use will be voluntary and free of charge.

This is a plan. The FAQ, checked on the same date, speaks more generally of a first version planned for early 2027, with further functions such as signatures and additional certificates introduced gradually from 2027. No changed date was found on 13 November 2026, but plans and available functions can change, so consult the programme’s current information before relying on a date.

The ecosystem

According to the programme, the wallet is part of an open, state-framed ecosystem in which companies, public authorities and organisations issue, integrate and use credentials within their existing processes. Its ecosystem page says use cases can already be implemented and tested in a production-like environment.

Where the information comes from

Responsible source: the German EUDI Wallet programme, an official project of the Federal Republic of Germany. According to its FAQ, the Federal Ministry for Digital Transformation and Government Modernisation (BMDS) is responsible for the technical implementation. The pages were checked on 13 November 2026.
